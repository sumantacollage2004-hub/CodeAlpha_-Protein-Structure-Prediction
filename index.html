Structural Analysis Report

Summary findings from computational structure prediction

1. Protein Selection & Rationale

The human tumor suppressor protein p53, encoded by the TP53 gene (UniProt accession P04637), was selected for this structural analysis. p53 is one of the most studied proteins in cancer biology — it is mutated in over 50% of all human cancers. Its multidomain architecture and the availability of both experimental and predicted structures make it an ideal candidate for computational structural analysis.

2. Structure Prediction Methodology

The 3D structure was retrieved from the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database (Model ID: AF-P04637-F1, version 4). AlphaFold v2 uses an attention-based neural network architecture to predict atomic-resolution structures from amino acid sequences, trained on the PDB and UniRef90. For validation, a homology model was also generated using SWISS-MODEL using PDB template 2OCJ (full-length p53 tetramer, resolution 2.05 Å) with a GMQE score of 0.82, indicating high model quality.

3. Overall Structural Architecture

The full-length p53 monomer (393 residues) is a mosaic of structured and disordered regions. The N-terminal transactivation domain (residues 1–67) is largely intrinsically disordered in isolation, as reflected by low pLDDT scores (<50) in this region of the AlphaFold model. The proline-rich region (68–98) adopts a loose PPII helix arrangement. The DNA-binding domain (99–292) is the most structured region (pLDDT > 90), forming an immunoglobulin-like β-sandwich with four β-strands on each face, supporting the loop-sheet-helix motif that contacts DNA. The tetramerization domain (293–356) forms a compact helical bundle, and the C-terminal regulatory domain (357–393) is again disordered.

4. Key Structural Features

DNA-Binding Interface: The L2 (residues 164–194) and L3 (237–250) loops, along with the loop-sheet-helix (LSH) motif, form the primary DNA-contacting surface. Arg248 inserts into the DNA minor groove, while Arg273, Ala276, Cys277, and Arg280 contact the DNA backbone phosphates. The structural zinc (coordinated by Cys176–His179–Cys238–Cys242) stabilizes the L2/L3 architecture.

Tetramerization: p53 functions as a dimer-of-dimers. Each monomer contributes a β-strand (residues 326–333) and a long α-helix (335–354). Two monomers form a primary dimer via antiparallel β-sheet, and two dimers associate through helix–helix contacts to form the biologically active tetramer.

Electrostatic Surface: The DNA-binding face shows a marked positive electrostatic potential (Lys, Arg residues), complementary to the negatively charged DNA phosphate backbone. The opposite face is predominantly negative and hydrophobic, contributing to protein–protein interactions.

5. Confidence Assessment

AlphaFold's per-residue confidence scores (pLDDT) reveal high confidence (>90) for the DBD core (residues 120–280) and the tetramerization domain helix (335–354). The disordered N- and C-terminal regions score <50, consistent with their experimentally confirmed disorder. The linker regions (residues 292–325, between DBD and TET) score 50–70, indicating moderate confidence — these flexible linkers are notoriously difficult to predict. Overall, the model quality is excellent for the structured domains and appropriately uncertain for the disordered regions.

6. Conclusions

AlphaFold v2 provides an exceptionally accurate model of the p53 structured domains in agreement with experimental crystallographic data. The predicted structure confirms the modular domain architecture, clearly delineates the DNA-binding and tetramerization interfaces, and correctly identifies the intrinsically disordered regulatory regions. This model can be used as a basis for drug discovery targeting the MDM2-binding helix, the DNA-contacting loops, and the tetramer interface — all critical targets in cancer therapy.
